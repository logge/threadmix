A DJ that runs itself.

Point ThreadMix at your music. It listens to every track, then plays one continuous mix: it picks what comes next, matches tempo and key, and blends each record into the next the way a DJ would. All night, on your own computer.

What it does

Everything a DJ does between records, and some things they don't: the choosing, the mixing, the talking, and a light show.

Mixes like a DJ

Beatmatched, key-aware blends. Splits songs into vocals, drums, bass and the rest, for acapella blends, drum swaps and mixes that never talk over the singing.

Talk to it

Type "keep it deep", "take it to peak time" or "plan a three-hour party that starts chilled", and it shapes the night to match.

A voice on the mic

Between songs the DJ says a line out loud, over the music, and has something to say about the record that's playing.

Knows the words

Transcribes lyrics, for a lyrics visualizer and a keep-it-clean mode that skips explicit songs.

A light show

More than a dozen visualizers that move with the music, and a party view big enough to read from across the room.

Stays on your computer

Your music and everything the AI does stay on your machine. The models download once; nothing you play is sent anywhere.

How it works

Four steps, and the first one only happens once.

  1. Choose your music folderThreadMix scans it in the background for tempo, key, beats, energy and where the vocals are. The first scan takes minutes for a few albums and hours for a big collection, but you can start as soon as the first tracks are done.
  2. Press Start setThe DJ picks the records, beatmatches them and mixes them in. Thumbs up or down on a track steers what it plays.
  3. Talk to itAsk for a mood, an energy, or a whole evening planned out.
  4. Press PartyBig visuals and what's playing, for a screen at the party.
The first launch asks for your music folder and offers the AI models, with what each one adds and how big it is.

Installing

Windows says "Windows protected your PC"

That's SmartScreen being careful with an app it hasn't seen many downloads of yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.

Running it on Linux

The Linux download is an AppImage: one file, nothing to install. Make it executable and start it, from the file manager (Properties > Permissions > Allow executing file as program, then double-click) or from a terminal:

chmod +x ThreadMix-Linux-x86_64.AppImage
./ThreadMix-Linux-x86_64.AppImage

If it says no suitable fusermount was found, install FUSE (sudo apt install fuse3, or fuse3 from your distribution), or start it with APPIMAGE_EXTRACT_AND_RUN=1 in front. It plays through your system's sound (PipeWire or PulseAudio), and downloads the models with your system's curl.

Which AI models should I download?

The welcome screen offers four, and ticks the ones that suit your computer:

  • A better DJ (680 MB): beat tracking, stem separation and a sound fingerprint. Worth it for everyone.
  • Lyrics (150 MB): for the lyrics visualizer and keep-it-clean.
  • A voice for the DJ (350 MB): so it talks out loud.
  • The DJ's brain (6.7 GB): chat, party planning and what it says between songs. Google's Gemma model, so you accept Google's Gemma terms to download it.

You can change your mind later in Settings > Manage models.

Where does it keep things?

Your library file and the separated stems go in a stems folder inside your music folder. On Windows the models go in %LOCALAPPDATA%\BeatMix\models, and the settings in %APPDATA%\BeatMix. Uninstalling from Windows Settings removes the app; delete those folders too to remove everything. On Linux both go in ~/.config/BeatMix; to remove everything, delete the AppImage and that folder.

ThreadMix's own code is under the GNU GPL v2 or later. The app is distributed under the GNU AGPL v3 (it is built with JUCE), and its library scanner under the GNU GPL v2 (it uses QM-DSP). It also includes llama.cpp, whisper.cpp, ONNX Runtime, Signalsmith Stretch and libebur128 (MIT), espeak-ng (GPL v3 or later), libvorbis (BSD), dr_libs, stb and SQLite (public domain), and on Windows NVIDIA's CUDA and cuDNN runtime libraries under NVIDIA's redistribution terms. The full list is in the licenses folder next to the app on Windows, inside the AppImage on Linux, and in the source code.
